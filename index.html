      About Tubig

      Tubig (Filipino for "water") is a working prototype by Gregor Posadas, a PhD student in Environmental Engineering at UC Berkeley, built for DevEng 203: Digital Transformation of Development (Fall 2026).

      Why it exists

      A water utility can pass every audit and still leave households without water. In 2024, the state auditor found Mabalacat City Water District met its water-quality target at every sampling point, while its operator logged 1,490 complaints, 84% about no water or low pressure.

      Complaints go from households to the operator. Audits go from the Commission on Audit to the district board to LWUA. The two never meet. Tubig connects them: it shows residents the rules, records their experience against those rules, and routes the gap to the office that can act on it.

      Questions

      Is my information stored?

      No. There are no accounts and nothing you type is sent to us. Your progress is saved only in this browser so you can come back to it; "Start over" clears it. When you pick a place, its name (barangay, city, province) is sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service to show it on the map. "Use my location" asks your browser for your position and sends it to the same service to find your barangay.

      Is this legal advice?

      No. Rules are summarized from an inventory of 165 codified obligations. Some are marked "text not yet confirmed" because the official text has not been read yet. Always check the linked source.

      Why does it say "no rule found" for hours and pressure?

      Because none was found. Water districts, which serve most towns outside Metro Manila, have no binding minimum for hours of supply or water pressure. Each district sets its own target. Tubig shows the national target for comparison and helps you ask the district for its own.

      How do you know who my provider is?

      We match your city or municipality to the 554 water districts in our crosswalk of LWUA, NWRB and COA records. Some districts serve several towns, and some towns have more than one provider, so you can always change the provider type.

      Are the contact details current?

      They come from official websites and Citizen's Charters and can change. Call to confirm before you rely on them.
