      The data behind Tubig

      About Tubig

      Tubig (Filipino for "water") is a working prototype built by Gregor Posadas, a PhD student at UC Berkeley, for DevEng 203: Digital Transformation of Development (Fall 2026).

      Why it exists

      A water utility can pass every audit and still leave households without water. In 2024, Mabalacat City Water District met its water-quality target at every sampling point while its operator logged 1,490 complaints, 84% of them about no water or low pressure. Complaints go from households to the operator. Audits go from the state auditor to the district board to LWUA. The two never meet. Tubig connects them: it shows residents the rules, records their experience against those rules, and routes the gap to the office that can act on it.

      Privacy

      • There are no accounts, and nothing you type is saved, stored or sent to us.
      • When you pick a place, its name (barangay, city, province) is sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service to find it on the map. Your street address is never asked for.
      • Your letter is written in your browser. It goes nowhere unless you send it yourself.

      Limits

      • This is not legal advice. Rules are summarized from the author's inventory of 165 codified obligations; some are marked "not yet confirmed" because the official text has not been read.
      • Provider matching is by city or municipality name. Some districts serve several towns, and some towns have more than one provider. You can change the provider type at any time.
      • Contact details come from official websites and Citizen's Charters and can change.
